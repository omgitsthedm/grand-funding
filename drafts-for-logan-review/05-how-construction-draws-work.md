# How construction draws work

*Draft for Logan's review. Business-purpose loans for real estate investors only. Not for owner-occupied or consumer purposes.*

## The basic idea

On a construction or major renovation loan, the money for the work usually isn't handed over all at once. It's released in stages — called **draws** — as the work gets done. This protects both the investor and the lender: funds follow progress.

## How a draw usually works

1. **Work gets done.** Your contractor completes a stage of the project.
2. **You request a draw.** You submit a request for the funds that cover that completed work.
3. **The work is checked.** The completed work is verified, often by an inspection.
4. **Funds are released.** Once the request is approved, money for that stage is released.

## What determines your draw schedule

There is no one-size-fits-all schedule. Grand Funding sets the draw structure for each project after review, based on:

- The plans and permits
- The construction budget and scope of work
- The contractor and the project's current status
- What work is already complete

The draw structure, milestones, inspections, and timing are disclosed in writing for your specific project.

## How to keep draws moving

- Keep an itemized budget and update it as work progresses.
- Make sure your contractor documents completed work clearly.
- Ask up front what each draw request must include and what costs must be paid before each draw.
- Plan for overruns. Know how you'd cover a cost increase before it happens.
