# What is a bridge loan, and when do investors use one?

*Draft for Logan's review. Business-purpose loans for real estate investors only. Not for owner-occupied or consumer purposes.*

## The short answer

A bridge loan is short-term financing that covers a gap. The gap is usually time: you need to act on a property now, and the money that will pay for it long term isn't ready yet.

The loan is secured by real estate and is meant to be paid off by a specific event — a sale, a refinance, or another source of repayment you can point to.

## When investors use one

- **Buying before selling.** You found the next property, but your current investment property hasn't sold yet.
- **A short contract deadline.** The seller wants certainty and won't wait for a slow bank process.
- **Bank financing fell through.** The original lender backed out close to closing.
- **Stabilizing a property.** The property needs work or new tenants before it qualifies for long-term financing.

## What a bridge loan is not

It is not long-term financing. If you plan to hold a stabilized rental for years, a bridge loan is the step that gets you there, not the destination. Plan the payoff before you borrow.

## The questions we'll ask

1. What is the property, and what is it worth today?
2. What debt is already on it?
3. What event will repay the bridge loan?
4. What happens if that sale or refinance takes longer than planned?
