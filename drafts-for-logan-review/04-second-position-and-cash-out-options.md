# Second-position and cash-out options for investors

*Draft for Logan's review. Business-purpose loans for real estate investors only. Not for owner-occupied or consumer purposes.*

If you own an investment property with equity in it, there are two common ways to put that equity to work. They solve the same problem in different ways.

## Option 1: Cash-out refinance

A cash-out refinance **replaces** your existing first mortgage with a new, larger loan. The old loan is paid off, and you receive the difference in cash.

- You end up with one loan instead of two.
- Your existing loan's terms go away, and the new loan's terms take their place.
- Title work and a payoff from your current lender are part of the process.

## Option 2: Second-position loan

A second-position loan (also called a junior lien or second mortgage) **adds** a new loan behind your existing first mortgage. The first mortgage stays in place, unchanged.

- You keep your current first loan.
- You'll have two payments and two lenders.
- The first mortgage documents matter: some limit additional borrowing.

## How to compare them

Ask the same questions for both options:

1. How much cash will you actually receive after existing debt and costs?
2. What are the combined monthly payments?
3. What fees and closing costs apply?
4. When is each loan due, and how will you pay it off?
5. What does your current first mortgage allow?

The right choice depends on your existing loan, the property, and what you plan to do with the money.
