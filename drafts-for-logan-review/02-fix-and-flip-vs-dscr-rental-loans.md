# Fix-and-flip vs. DSCR rental loans

*Draft for Logan's review. Business-purpose loans for real estate investors only. Not for owner-occupied or consumer purposes.*

## Two different plans, two different loans

The right loan depends on what you plan to do with the property after you buy it.

| | Fix-and-flip loan | DSCR rental loan |
|---|---|---|
| **The plan** | Buy, renovate, sell | Buy or refinance, rent, hold |
| **How it's repaid** | Sale of the finished property | Rental income over time, then sale or refinance |
| **Loan length** | Short-term | Long-term |
| **What drives the review** | Purchase price, repair budget, after-repair value, and your exit | The property's rental income compared with its debt payments |

## Fix-and-flip loans

A fix-and-flip loan is short-term financing for buying a property that needs work and selling it after the renovation. The review focuses on the property, the scope of work, the budget, and recent sales that support the after-repair value (ARV).

Grand Funding offers fix-and-flip loans for investment properties in Arizona and California.

## DSCR rental loans

"DSCR" stands for debt service coverage ratio: the property's rental income compared with the loan payments. A DSCR loan is a long-term option for investors who want to hold a rental. Many investors use a short-term loan first, then refinance into long-term financing once the property is renovated and rented.

Grand Funding's products are short-term business-purpose loans. If your long-term plan is a rental, talk with Logan about how the short-term loan fits with the refinance you plan to use afterward.

## How to choose

- Selling after the renovation? Start with a fix-and-flip conversation.
- Keeping it as a rental? Plan the long-term refinance before you buy, and ask how short-term financing fits in between.
- Not sure yet? Bring both versions of the plan. Logan will ask which one the numbers support.
